Privacy

For My Films by OneLifeStack. This is the same text the app shows under Settings › Privacy.

My Films is a video editor that runs entirely on your phone. This page says what the app can see and what it does with it.

What stays on your phone

  • Your videos, photos and music. The app reads them from where they already are to show them, play them and render your film. It never copies them to a server, and it never changes the originals.
  • Your projects. Each project is a small file inside the app's private storage. It lists which clips you used and the edits you made. Deleting the app deletes these files.
  • Your exported films. Saved to your gallery under Movies › My Films. They are ordinary video files you own; deleting the app leaves them alone.
  • Thumbnails. Small frames of your clips are cached in the app's private storage to draw the timeline quickly. Settings › Erase everything removes them.

What leaves your phone

Nothing. The app does not request the internet permission, so it cannot send anything anywhere. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising.

Permissions

  • Videos and photos (READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, or READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE on older Android): to show your gallery in the picker. Optional: the system picker works without it.
  • Selected photos (READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED): on Android 14 and newer you can share only the items you choose.
  • Music (READ_MEDIA_AUDIO): to list your songs when you add music. Optional: a file picker works without it.
  • Notifications: to show export progress and tell you when a film is ready.
  • Foreground service (media processing on Android 15+, data sync on Android 14), wake lock: to keep an export running when you leave the app. Nothing is synced anywhere; "data sync" is just the name of the service type Android 14 offers.
  • Write storage (Android 9 and older only): to save the exported film to the gallery.

A library the app uses (Media3) declares ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE. The app has no INTERNET permission, so it still cannot go online.

Sharing

When you tap Share, Android's share sheet hands the exported file to the app you choose. What that app does with it is covered by its own policy.

Changes

If a future version adds anything that leaves the phone, this page and the Play listing will say so first, and it will be opt-in.
